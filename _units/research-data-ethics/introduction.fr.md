Historians have long engaged with questions of provenance, reliability and the accuracy of their sources. Ethical concerns are equally central to historical practice, particularly when working with living individuals, collective memories or oral histories. Data ethics combines the two aspects, referring to the principles and responsibilities involved in using and disseminating such material.

What distinguishes the digital age is the amplification of these issues. Digital technologies enable data to circulate at unprecedented scale and speed, to be recombined across contexts and to reach audiences far beyond those originally envisaged, raising new ethical stakes related to control, context and consent.

### More about the lesson
<!-- section-contents -->

This lesson works through these dimensions in three assignments. The first introduces the core principles of data ethics – consent, ownership and integrity – through a born-digital oral history case study. The second maps these principles onto the full research data lifecycle, using a data management plan exercise drawn from a local history project. The third applies the FAIR and CARE principles as practical frameworks for responsible data sharing and reuse.
